# Adoption Guide

S.P.I.N. should feel like a clearer way to write a message, not a new approval process.

## Before rollout

- Map Critical, High, Normal, and Low to existing severity guidance.
- Choose two or three channels or workflows for the trial.
- Publish the plain-text and tool-specific templates.
- Identify a lightweight feedback route.
- Tell teams explicitly that Subject and Priority are the only mandatory fields.

## Four-week rollout

### Week 1 — Introduce

- Explain the communication problem and the four fields.
- Share templates and priority definitions.
- Walk through one routine defect and one genuine incident.
- Reinforce that established incident and security procedures still apply.

### Week 2 — Encourage

- Encourage use in selected engineering channels.
- Gather examples of unclear guidance.
- Coach through examples rather than policing formatting.
- Avoid adding approval gates or mandatory metadata.

### Week 3 — Refine

- Improve examples based on real questions, using fictionalised data in public material.
- Clarify local priority mappings and ownership conventions.
- Review whether recipients can understand requests with less follow-up.

### Week 4 — Decide

- Ask participating teams whether the convention improved clarity.
- Identify friction, misuse, or duplicated workflow.
- Decide whether to adopt more broadly, revise, or stop the trial.

## What to observe

Use qualitative evidence before inventing metrics. Look for clearer ownership, fewer follow-up questions about impact, better distinction between routine reports and escalation, and correct activation of existing incident processes.

Do not turn S.P.I.N. compliance into a performance measure. The framework exists to support collaboration.
